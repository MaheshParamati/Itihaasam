# Source Texts (public domain)

Use these as reference/context when writing scripts. Do not copy prose verbatim into scripts (paraphrase for scripts) — these are for factual/narrative accuracy, not final copy. Copyrighted retellings (Amar Chitra Katha, Rajagopalachari) can inform tone/pacing but must not be copied.

## Ramayana

- **Hari Prasad Shastri translation** (modern, readable prose, 3 vols, all 7 Kandas) — [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/The.Ramayana.of.Valmiki.by.Hari.Prasad.Shastri)
- **Manmatha Nath Dutt translation** (prose, 7 vols, 1891–94) — [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/Valmiki_Ramayana_English_Prose_Translation_7_volumes_by_Manmatha_Nath_Dutt_1891_to_1894)
- **Ralph T. H. Griffith translation** (verse) — [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/24869)
- Sanskrit + English side-by-side (Gita Press) — [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/valmiki-ramayana-gita-press-english)
- Chapter-indexed online reading copy — [valmikiramayan.net](https://www.valmikiramayan.net/)

Recommended primary: **Shastri translation** for readability when mining stories; Griffith when you want more poetic/dramatic phrasing for narration.

## Mahabharata

- **Kisari Mohan Ganguli translation** — the only complete public-domain English translation, all 18 parvas. Originally scanned/hosted at sacred-texts.com; also on:
  - [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/TheMahabharataOfKrishna-dwaipayanaVyasa)
  - [Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Mahabharat) (Books 1–4 proofed)
  - [Full-text archive, single-page reading version](https://www.fulltextarchive.com/book/The-Mahabharata-of-Krishna-Dwaipayana/)

Public domain for any use (attribution appreciated, not required).
